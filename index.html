Files

Queue
Add several files to transcode them one after another. Each file keeps the settings that were set when it was added.

Library scan
What's worth converting? Reads every video in a folder (only the file information; nothing is changed), estimates how much space converting each one to HEVC would save, and points out files that are likely to make Plex convert them while you watch.
Estimates assume x265 at very good quality (constant quality around 18–20); grain and dark films come out bigger. A 30-second test shows the real result for one file. "Worth converting" means at least 300 MB and a quarter of the file saved.

Source
A profile keeps the Video settings, the Audio and Subtitles rules and the Advanced overrides, and can name the converted files after itself. Not included: the files and folders, one file's own track choices and metadata, PC settings, and "Replace the original".

Video
GPU encoders: no HDR10+, and the quality number is a different scale than x265 (the same value gives a bigger file). Try a short clip first.
Slower = smaller file at the same quality
Smaller = much faster encode and smaller file. HDR10, HDR10+ and Dolby Vision are kept.
Cleans up noise and compression fizz; also makes files smaller. Light keeps film grain recognisable.
Lower = better quality, bigger file. 16–20 is visually transparent for most 4K HDR.
For example 20M or 8000k
Try your settings before converting the whole film: Test 30 seconds converts only 30 seconds (from the time you choose, or 40% into the film) and saves two short files where the result would go: "… (30 s test)", those seconds converted with these settings, and "… (30 s original)", the same seconds from the original, unchanged. Play them one after the other, or side by side, to compare the picture, the sound and the size. Change a setting and test again (the clips are replaced), and press Start when you are happy. The test clips are not needed after that.
